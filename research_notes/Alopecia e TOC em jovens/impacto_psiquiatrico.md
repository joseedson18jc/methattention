# Impacto psiquiátrico/psicológico da alopecia em homens jovens e segurança psiquiátrica dos tratamentos (finasterida/dutasterida/minoxidil)

Notes compiled 2026-10-21. Sources: PubMed (articles cited with DOI links), EMA, FDA (via secondary reports), ANVISA (via reposted alert copies). Notes in English; final report in Portuguese.

## 1. Depression/anxiety prevalence and quality of life in androgenetic alopecia (AGA) and alopecia areata (AA); effect of young age of onset

### Takeaway
Alopecia areata has a consistent link to depression and anxiety. Pooled ORs are about 2.5–3 for symptoms, and diagnosed disorders are lower (OR about 1.4–1.7; prevalence 7–17%). For androgenetic alopecia in men the evidence is weaker and mixed. QoL impairment is moderate (pooled DLQI about 8), and anxiety, social anxiety and stress symptoms are raised. Some meta-analyses find no depression signal in men. Early onset (before 20) and severe or self-rated severe loss track with more distress.

### Cited Findings
**Alopecia areata (AA)**
- Okhovat et al. 2019 meta-analysis (8 studies; 6,010 AA patients vs 20,961 controls; 4 cross-sectional and 4 case-control): pooled OR for anxiety 2.50 (95% CI 1.54–4.06) and for depression 2.71 (1.52–4.82). The authors recommend routine assessment and referral. Publication bias is possible. — [J Am Acad Dermatol 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.jaad.2019.05.086](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2019.05.086)
- Lauron et al. 2023, JAMA Dermatology (37 articles; 29 on depression, 26 on anxiety): this review separated diagnosed disorders from questionnaire symptoms.
  - Depressive disorders: prevalence 9%, OR 1.38. Specific anxiety disorders: prevalence 7–17%, OR 1.51–1.69. Unspecified anxiety disorders: 13%.
  - Depressive symptoms: prevalence 37%, OR 2.70. Anxiety symptoms: prevalence 34%, OR 3.07.
  - Conclusion: 7–17% have disorders needing psychiatric care, and more than a third have warning-sign symptoms that need monitoring. Variation between studies was driven mainly by methodology.
  — [JAMA Dermatol 2023, DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.6085](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.6085)
- Front Med 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis (93 studies; children and adults): adults with AA have significantly more anxiety and depression symptoms than healthy controls, with moderate QoL impact. Outcomes are often similar to those of other dermatologic patients. — [Front Med 2022, DOI 10.3389/fmed.2022.1054898](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2022.1054898)
- Rencz et al. 2016, BJD meta-analysis (21 studies; 2,530 adults with AA): pooled DLQI 6.3 (95% CI 5.6–7.1). SF-36 scores were significantly lower in the role-emotional, mental health and vitality domains vs matched controls (p<0.001). Scalp involvement, anxiety and depression worsened QoL, and wig use improved it. — [Br J Dermatol 2016, DOI 10.1111/bjd.14497](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjd.14497)
- Bidirectional Mendelian randomization (FinnGen; 682 AA cases): genetic liability to AA was associated with major depressive disorder (OR 1.01) and anxiety (OR 1.16). There was no reverse effect: depression and anxiety did not increase AA risk. — [J Affect Disord 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.152](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.152)
- In the ASAMI eDelphi global expert consensus, a "history of anxiety, depression, or suicidal ideation due to or exacerbated by AA" counts as a determinant of AA severity, beyond the SALT score. — [JAMA Dermatol 2024, DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2023.5869](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2023.5869)

**Androgenetic alopecia (AGA)**
- Huang et al. 2021, JAMA Dermatology (41 studies; 7,995 patients):
  - Pooled DLQI 8.16 (95% CI 5.62–10.71), meaning moderate impairment.
  - Hair-Specific Skindex-29 emotion domain: 29.22 (24.17–34.28), also moderate.
  - Pooled CES-D 14.98 (14.28–15.68), which is below the depression cutoff, so no association with depressive symptoms was found.
  - Worse HRQoL was linked to higher self-rated severity, a lower VAS score and higher education. Better HRQoL was linked to being married or partnered and to receiving treatment.
  — [JAMA Dermatol 2021, DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.2196](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.2196)
- Frith & Jankowski 2023 (men only; 37 studies): the meta-analysis found no impact on depression (pooled 8.8, 95% CI 6.8–10.8) and a moderate impact on QoL (pooled 9.12, 6.14–12.10). Men with AGA had average or better mental health than men without AGA.
  - Caveats: conflicts of interest were probable in 78% of studies and samples were biased in 68%.
  - Authors' advice: do not overstate distress, and screen with validated tools that have clear cutoffs.
  — [Psychol Health Med 2023, DOI 10.1080/13548506.2023.2242049](https://doi.org/10.1080/13548506.2023.2242049)
- Kong et al. 2025 (13 studies; 2,737 AGA patients vs 17,382 controls): AGA patients had more of the following than controls:
  - generalized anxiety symptoms (SMD 0.50, borderline p=0.05)
  - social anxiety symptoms (SMD 0.50, 95% CI 0.16–0.84, p=0.004)
  - depressive symptoms (SMD 0.38, p=0.004)
  - perceived stress (SMD 1.09, p<0.001)
  - There was no difference in general distress. Qualitative findings included lower self-esteem and life satisfaction and more body-image dissatisfaction and interpersonal sensitivity.
  — [Front Psychiatry 2025, DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1705957](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1705957)
- Visible-skin-disease meta-analysis (41 studies; 12,372 patients, including AA): pooled depression prevalence 38.0% and anxiety 43.6%, with high heterogeneity (I² >97%). Suicidal ideation ranged from 5.8% to 23.3%. — [Ann Dermatol Venereol 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.annder.2026.103511](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annder.2026.103511)

**Young age of onset**
- Turkish cohort (n=390 AGA patients): mean onset was 23.9 years in men. Men had significantly more emotional and functional distress than women. Onset before age 20 was associated with higher emotion, function and stigma scores and lower self-confidence (p=0.007, <0.001, 0.037 and <0.001). Social media use (69.3%) did not change distress or treatment choice. — [J Cosmet Dermatol 2025, DOI 10.1111/jocd.70167](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70167)
- Scoping review of early-onset AGA (65 studies; 78% male-only): young men with AGA tended to have more anxiety and lower self-esteem than men without hair loss. — [PLoS One 2024, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0299212](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0299212)
- A review of psychosomatic aspects of AA (Brazilian author) names women, young patients, those with extensive AA and those with prior psychiatric disorders as the higher-risk groups. — [Clin Dermatol 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.clindermatol.2018.08.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clindermatol.2018.08.011)

### Inferences
- The "distress" signal in AGA is real but mostly sub-clinical: QoL, anxiety, social anxiety and self-image are affected. Major depression is not clearly increased at population level. The burden is concentrated in younger men, men with early or fast onset and men who rate their loss as severe.
- AA carries a heavier psychiatric burden than AGA. Over a third of AA patients have clinically relevant symptoms.

### Gaps
- No Hairdex data specific to young men were retrieved.
- No meta-analytic OR for diagnosed depression in AGA vs controls was found. Evidence for men is conflicting (Huang/Frith show no depression signal; Kong shows small-to-moderate increases in symptoms).

## 2. Self-esteem, body image, social anxiety, perceived attractiveness/age, workplace and dating impacts (male-specific)

### Takeaway
Observer studies show that hair affects how old and how attractive men look. Studies of men themselves show lower self-esteem and more social anxiety and body-image dissatisfaction. Hard data on employment or dating outcomes are scarce.

### Cited Findings
- Bater et al. 2016, randomized photo experiment (122 observers; 7 men photographed before and after hair transplant, plus 6 controls): after transplant, men were rated 3.6 years younger (SD 2.9; p<.001). They were also rated more attractive (58.5/100; p<.001), more successful (57.1; p=.008) and more approachable (59.2; p=.02). The authors link these domains to workplace and social success. — [JAMA Facial Plast Surg 2016, DOI 10.1001/jamafacial.2016.0546](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamafacial.2016.0546)
- AGA meta-analysis (Kong 2025): higher social anxiety symptoms (SMD 0.50), lower self-esteem and more body-image dissatisfaction. — [DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1705957](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1705957)
- Huang 2021: being single and higher self-rated severity predicted worse HRQoL. — [DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.2196](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.2196)
- Survey in 17 European countries (5,487 dermatology outpatients and 2,808 controls): alopecia was among the most stigmatized conditions. Perceived stigmatization was greater with lower age, **male sex**, being single, suicidal ideation, body dysmorphic concerns and low appearance satisfaction. — [Acta Derm Venereol 2023, DOI 10.2340/actadv.v103.6485](https://doi.org/10.2340/actadv.v103.6485)

### Inferences
- In young single men, alopecia may interact with appearance anxiety and body dysmorphic concerns, which is relevant to the OCD-spectrum theme of the parent report. A history of body dysmorphic disorder or OCD should be checked when distress is out of proportion to the clinical loss.

### Gaps
- No robust quantitative studies on workplace or dating outcomes for real men with AGA were retrieved; the evidence is mainly observer-rating experiments.

## 3. Bidirectionality: stress or depression triggering telogen effluvium or AA flares

### Takeaway
Stress is a commonly reported trigger for AA relapse and for telogen effluvium (TE), and a plausible one (HPA axis, neurogenic inflammation). Most of this evidence is observational or retrospective. Genetic (Mendelian randomization) data suggest AA→depression rather than depression→AA.

### Cited Findings
- Retrospective study of patchy AA (n=256): stress/fatigue was the most common relapse trigger, along with illness, thyroid disease and seasonal change. — [Dermatol Online J 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30677822/)
- Paediatric TE (n=76): a trigger was identified in 67% of cases, most often emotional stress or febrile illness. — [Pediatr Dermatol 2022, DOI 10.1111/pde.15154](https://doi.org/10.1111/pde.15154)
- Rebora's TE review: "autoimmune" TE shares with AA a triggering role for emotional stress and frequently involves anxiety and trichodynia. — [G Ital Dermatol Venereol 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24566565/)
- AA psychosomatic review: the relationship is reciprocal, since psychiatric disorders can trigger AA onset and AA can worsen psychiatric state. — [Clin Dermatol 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.clindermatol.2018.08.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clindermatol.2018.08.011)
- Systematic review of 17 studies: hair loss is heavily associated with major depressive disorder and anxiety, with the HPA axis and the immune milieu around the follicle as the proposed mechanism. There were too few quantitative data to link each subtype to specific disorders. — [Skin Health Dis 2022, DOI 10.1002/ski2.194](https://doi.org/10.1002/ski2.194)
- Systematic review of sleep and hair loss (29 studies, Tosti group): sleep disturbance (PSQI) is consistently raised in AA, AGA and TE. The relationship looks bidirectional, and the authors suggest brief sleep screening. — [Dermatol Ther 2026, DOI 10.1007/s13555-025-01641-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-025-01641-6)
- Mendelian randomization: AA→MDD and AA→anxiety were significant, while anxiety/MDD→AA was not. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.152](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.152)

### Inferences
- The clinical message is a vicious cycle: stress can precipitate TE or an AA flare, and hair loss feeds back into anxiety and depression. Genetic evidence does not support depression causing AA.

### Gaps
- No prospective cohort quantifying the risk of an AA flare after depression episodes in young men was found.

## 4. Suicidal ideation related to alopecia itself

### Takeaway
AA is associated with a markedly higher risk of suicide attempt in one large Taiwanese cohort, independent of psychiatric comorbidity. Across visible skin diseases, suicidal ideation ranges from about 6% to 23%. Specific data for AGA are lacking.

### Cited Findings
- Taiwan national cohort (10,515 AA patients vs 105,150 controls): adjusted HR for suicide attempt 6.28 (95% CI 4.47–8.81). Risk stayed raised after stratifying by psychiatric disorders, and first suicidal behaviour occurred at a younger age. — [Dermatology 2023, DOI 10.1159/000530076](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530076)
- Visible skin diseases (including AA): suicidal ideation 5.8–23.3%. — [DOI 10.1016/j.annder.2026.103511](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annder.2026.103511)
- Ethics review (2026) argues that routine depression and distress screening is reasonable in AA care. Formal suicide-risk assessment should follow a positive screen rather than being given to everyone, inside a defined response pathway (assessment, safety measures, crisis resources, referral). Population studies show raised suicide-related risk but heterogeneous findings. — [Clin Dermatol 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.clindermatol.2026.09.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clindermatol.2026.09.024)

### Inferences
- The Taiwanese HR of 6.28 is very high and comes from a single administrative-data study, so it should be presented as a strong signal, not a definitive estimate.

### Gaps
- No study of suicidality in men with AGA alone, without drug exposure, was found. AGA-related suicidality data are confounded by finasteride.

## 5. Psychiatric safety of finasteride/dutasteride (regulatory and epidemiological), post-finasteride syndrome, minoxidil

### Takeaway
Regulators now list suicidal ideation as a side effect of oral finasteride:
- EMA/PRAC confirmed it in May 2025, with an EC decision in August 2025.
- ANVISA issued an alert in June 2025.
- The FDA reportedly added a label warning in June 2022 and issued an alert on compounded topical finasteride in April 2025.

The evidence splits two ways. Pharmacovigilance shows strong disproportionality signals, especially in men aged 18–45 treated for hair loss. Large cohorts and meta-analyses (mostly older men with BPH) show raised depression risk but no clear increase in completed suicide. Dutasteride shows a weaker or absent signal.

### Cited Findings
**Regulatory**
- **EMA (Article 31 referral):**
  - Timeline: started 3 Oct 2024; PRAC recommendation 8 May 2025; CMDh 19 Jun 2025; EC decision 22 Aug 2025.
  - Suicidal ideation was confirmed as a side effect of finasteride 1 mg and 5 mg tablets, frequency unknown. Most cases involved 1 mg (hair loss).
  - Topical finasteride spray: no evidence of a link, so no change.
  - Dutasteride: a link was not established, but mood-change and suicidal-ideation warnings were added as a precaution (class effect).
  - Data: 325 EudraVigilance cases (313 finasteride, 13 dutasteride, 1 both) against about 270 million patient-years of finasteride exposure and about 82 million of dutasteride.
  - Measures: a patient card in finasteride 1 mg packs, advice to seek help for sexual dysfunction (which may contribute to mood changes), and a DHPC letter.
  - Advice for 1 mg users: stop treatment and seek medical advice if depressed mood, depression or suicidal ideation occurs.
  - Benefit–risk remains positive for all approved uses.
  — [EMA referral page](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/referrals/finasteride-dutasteride-containing-medicinal-products); [PRAC highlights May 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/meeting-highlights-pharmacovigilance-risk-assessment-committee-prac-5-8-may-2025)
- **ANVISA (Brazil), Alerta GGMON nº 7/2025 (June 2025):**
  - Prompted by the PRAC review. It cites increased risk of sexual dysfunction, mood changes, depression and suicidal ideation at usual doses of finasteride and dutasteride.
  - Benefit remains greater than risk, and companies must update package inserts. Products named include Finalop, Finastil, Hominus, Avodart and Dutam.
  - Professionals should inform patients and stop treatment if symptoms occur.
  - The Brazilian finasteride label already mentioned mood changes, depression and suicidal ideation.
  - Reportedly most cases involved 1 mg, including patients without prior psychiatric history.
  - Only reposted copies were seen, not the original on gov.br. — [Legismap repost of Anvisa alerts, 26/06/2025](https://legismap.com.br/conteudos/artigos-e-noticias/alertas-anvisa-em-26-06-2025); [Campo Verde-MT repost](https://campoverde.mt.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/15.-Alertas-e-Comunicados-de-Risco-27-06-2025.pdf)
- **FDA 2022 label:** reported that in June 2022 the FDA required a suicidal thoughts/behaviour warning on the Propecia label but denied the 2017 PFS Foundation petition to withdraw the drug. In 2011 the label had added only depression. More than 700 suicide or suicidal-ideation reports were made after 2011. The EMA required a suicidal-thoughts warning for 1 mg in 2017, and Health Canada in 2019. — [Fox Business/Reuters](https://www.foxbusiness.com/healthcare/merck-anti-baldness-drug-propecia-suicide). Confirmed in a peer-reviewed urology review that the FDA "recently added SI" to the adverse effects. — [Eur Urol Open Sci 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.euros.2023.04.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euros.2023.04.009). The exact DailyMed label text was not verified.
- **FDA compounded topical finasteride alert (22 Apr 2025):**
  - 32 FAERS reports (2019–2024). Reported effects included erectile dysfunction, anxiety, **suicidal ideation**, brain fog, depression, fatigue, insomnia, reduced libido and testicular pain, plus local irritation and a risk of transfer to others.
  - Most reports say the effects persisted after stopping. Some patients were told topical meant "no risk".
  - There is no FDA-approved topical finasteride. Products are sold by compounders and telehealth platforms, sometimes combined with minoxidil.
  — [FDA alert (official URL)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded); [Epocrates summary](https://epocrates.com/online/article/fda-warns-of-risks-with-compounded-topical-finasteride); [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/fda-alerts/1091-0.html). The official page returned 401 to our fetcher, so details come from secondary summaries.
- **Note on topical finasteride:** the EMA and FDA positions differ. The EMA found no evidence for the approved EU topical spray, while the FDA received reports for compounded topical products.

**Pharmacovigilance**
- Nguyen et al. 2021, JAMA Dermatology (VigiBase: 356 suicidality and 2,926 psychological adverse-event reports):
  - Suicidality ROR 1.63 (1.47–1.81); psychological adverse events ROR 4.33 (4.17–4.49).
  - Patients ≤45 years: suicidality ROR 3.47 (2.90–4.15). Alopecia indication: ROR 2.06 (1.81–2.34). No signal in older men with BPH.
  - Reporting rose after 2012 (ROR 2.13). The authors say this may reflect stimulated reporting or greater vulnerability in younger men.
  - 70.9% of reports with age data were from men aged 18–44.
  — [JAMA Dermatol 2021, DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2020.3385](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2020.3385)
- Lee et al. 2025 (VigiBase, more than 35 million reports):
  - Finasteride: suicidality ROR 7.28 (6.57–8.06); depression ROR 28.18 (26.57–29.89).
  - Dutasteride: no suicidality signal; weaker depression signal (ROR 3.23).
  - Signals were strongest at ages 18–44 (suicidality IC 3.54, depression IC 5.25).
  - Mean time to onset: about 115 days for suicidality and about 93 days for depression, mostly after 3 months of use.
  — [Eur J Clin Pharmacol 2025, DOI 10.1007/s00228-025-03851-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-025-03851-5)
- Gupta et al. 2025 (FAERS):
  - No signals in 2006–2011.
  - Suicidal ideation ROR 2.8 in 2013–2018 and 5.0 in 2019–2023.
  - No dutasteride signals in any period.
  - The authors attribute the rise partly to awareness after post-finasteride syndrome (PFS) was recognized in 2012, i.e. notoriety bias.
  — [J Cosmet Dermatol 2025, DOI 10.1111/jocd.70102](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70102)
- Thaibah 2025 (FAERS 2015–2024):
  - 43% of reports were from men aged 18–40.
  - Suicidality ROR was 27.5 in 2019, peaked at 34.6 in 2022 and was 19.0 in 2024.
  - Outcomes included disability (18.7%), life-threatening events (12.9%) and death (7.5%).
  — [Pharmaceuticals 2025, DOI 10.3390/ph18070957](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph18070957)

**Cohorts and meta-analyses**
- Swedish register cohort (Garcia-Argibay 2022; 2,236,876 men aged 50–90; 70,645 started finasteride and 8,774 dutasteride; median age at initiation 73):
  - Depression: finasteride HR 1.61 (1.48–1.75); dutasteride HR 1.68 (1.43–1.96). The risk was constant over time.
  - **Suicide: not associated** (finasteride HR 1.22, 0.99–1.49; dutasteride HR 0.98).
  — [JAMA Netw Open 2022, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.48135](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.48135)
- Welk 2017, Ontario (93,197 men aged ≥66 with BPH):
  - Suicide: HR 0.88 (not significant).
  - Self-harm: HR 1.88 in the first 18 months.
  - Depression: HR 1.94 in the first 18 months, then 1.22.
  - Absolute increases: 17 per 100,000 person-years for self-harm and 237 per 100,000 for depression.
  — [JAMA Intern Med 2017, DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.0089](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.0089)
- Uleri 2024 meta-analysis (5 studies; 2.2 million patients): depression aHR 1.30 (0.85–2.00) and suicide aHR 1.30 (0.65–2.61), both not significant. — [Eur Urol Focus 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.euf.2024.04.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euf.2024.04.009)
- Fraenza 2026 meta-analysis (9 observational studies; BPH and AGA): pooled HR 1.07 (0.76–1.51) for suicide and neuropsychiatric events, not significant, I² 59%. — [Front Urol 2026, DOI 10.3389/fruro.2026.1881635](https://doi.org/10.3389/fruro.2026.1881635)
- Pompili 2021 meta-analysis: depressive symptoms OR 2.14 (1.40–3.27). Crude suicidal ideation/behaviour was 21.2% vs 14.0%, and persistent sexual dysfunction 60.1%. These crude pooled rates come from heterogeneous sources and should be read cautiously. — [J Clin Psychopharmacol 2021, DOI 10.1097/JCP.0000000000001379](https://doi.org/10.1097/JCP.0000000000001379)
- Wang 2025 (UK Biobank Cox model plus Mendelian randomization): no association of finasteride with depression or suicide. — [J Cosmet Dermatol 2025, DOI 10.1111/jocd.70579](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70579)
- Critical review (Brezis 2025): 4 adverse-event-database analyses and 4 health-record studies (2017–2023) show increased depression, anxiety or suicidal behaviour. The authors argue for the precautionary principle given the cosmetic indication. — [J Clin Psychiatry 2025, DOI 10.4088/JCP.25nr15862](https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.25nr15862)
- Mechanism: 5α-reductase inhibition reduces neurosteroids (e.g. allopregnanolone; GABAergic signalling), and 5αR2 mediates stress-induced allopregnanolone in the male prefrontal cortex. — [Front Neuroendocrinol 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.yfrne.2026.101272](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yfrne.2026.101272). Repeated finasteride induced depression-like behaviour in rats. — [Behav Brain Res 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.bbr.2019.03.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2019.03.006)

**Post-finasteride syndrome (PFS)**
- PFS means persistent sexual, neurological, physical and mental symptoms during or after finasteride use. It is not universally recognized and has no evidence-based treatment. Traish (2020) argues it affects a subset of susceptible men. — [Fertil Steril 2020, DOI 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2019.11.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2019.11.030)
- Gupta 2021 review: depression was added to the FDA label in 2011, and PFS was named around 2012. — [J Dermatolog Treat 2021, DOI 10.1080/09546634.2021.1959506](https://doi.org/10.1080/09546634.2021.1959506)

**Minoxidil**
- Low-dose oral minoxidil (pooled individual patient data, 14 studies, n=442): hypertrichosis 24%, pedal edema 2%, postural hypotension 1.1%, heart-rate changes 1.3%. No psychiatric adverse effects were reported. — [Dermatol Ther 2020, DOI 10.1111/dth.14106](https://doi.org/10.1111/dth.14106)
- Nguyen 2021 used minoxidil as a comparator for alopecia in its VigiBase sensitivity analysis. The abstract implies the finasteride signal was specific to finasteride but does not give minoxidil's ROR. — [DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2020.3385](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2020.3385)

### Inferences
- The signal is concentrated in the population the parent report covers: young men taking 1 mg for hair loss. That group is under-represented in the cohort studies, which mostly involve older men with BPH. The null suicide results therefore do not fully reassure for young men.
- Disproportionality signals are inflated by notoriety and stimulated reporting after 2012, so they cannot establish causation or incidence. Even so, EMA, ANVISA and FDA all chose label warnings.
- Practical stance: discuss mood and sexual risks before starting finasteride, ask about psychiatric history, monitor especially in months 1–6 (median onset about 3–4 months), and stop the drug if mood symptoms appear.
- Compounded or telehealth topical finasteride should not be presented as "risk-free".

### Gaps
- The DailyMed/FDA label text and the exact 2022 date were not verified from a primary FDA document.
- The original ANVISA alert on gov.br was not accessed directly.
- No dedicated psychiatric pharmacovigilance study of minoxidil was found. Its psychiatric profile is assumed benign based only on the absence of reports in safety reviews.
- There is no RCT designed to assess psychiatric outcomes of finasteride in young men.

## 6. Practical implications: screening, referral, warning signs, Brazilian resources

### Takeaway
Guidance supports routine brief screening for depression and distress in alopecia care, especially AA, young patients and severe loss, and before and during finasteride. Positive screens should lead to suicide-risk assessment and referral. In Brazil, CVV 188 (free, 24 hours) and the CAPS/RAPS network are the main resources.

### Cited Findings
- Okhovat 2019 and Lauron 2023 recommend routine assessment for anxiety and depression in AA and referral when indicated. They also call for monitoring of the more than a third of patients with sub-threshold symptoms. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jaad.2019.05.086](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2019.05.086); [DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.6085](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.6085)
- Frith 2023: screen with validated measures that have clear clinical thresholds, and avoid overstating distress. — [DOI 10.1080/13548506.2023.2242049](https://doi.org/10.1080/13548506.2023.2242049)
- Taghizadeh 2026: proportionate approach. ### Inferences
- A pragmatic flow for a young man with alopecia:
  1. Run PHQ-2 or PHQ-9 (and GAD-7) at baseline.
  2. Before any 5-ARI, ask about psychiatric history and sexual function.
  3. Re-screen at about 3 and 6 months on finasteride, which matches the mean onset of about 3–4 months in pharmacovigilance.
  4. Any positive answer to PHQ-9 item 9 or any suicidal ideation should lead to same-day risk assessment and referral (CAPS or emergency services), CVV 188 information, and stopping finasteride.
- SAMU 192 and UPA are standard Brazilian emergency routes, but they were not sourced in this search.

### Gaps
- No source specific to dermatology or alopecia validates PHQ-9 cutoffs or specifies when to refer.
- PHQ-9 item-9 handling is standard psychiatric practice but was not sourced here.
- No official Ministry of Health (gov.br) page on RAPS/CAPS was retrieved, and the current number of CAPS is unverified.
- The VigiMed reporting channel was not confirmed.
