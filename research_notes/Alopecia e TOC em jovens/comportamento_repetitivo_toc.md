# Comportamento repetitivo de tocar/passar a mão no cabelo (a ponto de ferir o couro cabeludo) em homem jovem (~30 anos) com queda de cabelo

Notes in English (final report will be in Portuguese). Most sources found via PubMed; DOI links given. Research date: Oct 2026.

## 1. Differential diagnosis: which conditions explain constant hair touching/rubbing that injures the scalp?

### Takeaway
No single DSM label is named "hair touching." In a man with real hair loss, the two leading frames are (a) **Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD)**, where the touching is a *checking* ritual driven by appearance preoccupation (thinning hair/balding is a typical male BDD focus), and (b) a **body-focused repetitive behavior (BFRB)**, where the rubbing itself gives relief or feels automatic. When rubbing breaks the hair it is called **trichoteiromania**, a dermatologic diagnosis that can sit on top of androgenetic alopecia (AGA). OCD, lichen simplex chronicus/neurotic excoriations, and others stay in the differential.

### Cited Findings
**BDD**
- BDD is an OCD-related condition: an intense preoccupation with perceived physical flaws that others can't see. "Diagnosis requires the presence of repetitive behaviours or mental acts typically aimed at checking, correcting or concealing perceived flaws." It affects about 2% of adults, usually starts before 18, has no major gender gap in adults, and carries "considerable" suicide risk. — [Rück et al., Nat Rev Dis Primers 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41572-024-00577-z)
- In 200 people with BDD, **men were more likely than women to obsess about "thinning hair/balding,"** genitals and body build. Men had poorer global functioning and were more often unable to work, including on disability for BDD. Women did more mirror checking, camouflaging and skin picking. — [Phillips, Menard & Fay, Compr Psychiatry 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comppsych.2005.07.002)
- Skin, hair and nose are the most common BDD concerns. Typical behaviors are mirror checking, camouflaging (e.g., wearing a hat), skin picking, and **reassurance seeking, which may be enacted with surgeons and dermatologists**. BDD is linked to poor quality of life and a high rate of suicide attempts. — [Phillips & Dufresne, Am J Clin Dermatol 2000](https://doi.org/10.2165/00128071-200001040-00005)
- DSM-5 "Other specified obsessive-compulsive and related disorder" includes **"body dysmorphic-like disorder with actual flaws."** This covers BDD-like preoccupation when the flaw *is* clearly visible to others, which matters when hair loss is real. It also includes "BDD-like disorder without repetitive behaviors." — [PsychDB, citing DSM-5](https://psychdb.com/ocd/z-other-oc-and-related-disorders)
- AGA is common and tied to body image. A "borderline group" of hair-loss patients with body dysmorphic features should be identified and treated psychologically before hair transplant. — [Dhami, Indian J Plast Surg 2021](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0041-1741037)

**BFRBs / other specified OCRD**
- DSM-5 lists "body-focused repetitive behavior disorder" (e.g., nail biting, lip biting, cheek chewing, with repeated attempts to cut down) under Other Specified OCRD. It must not be better explained by trichotillomania, excoriation disorder, stereotypic movement disorder or non-suicidal self-injury. — [PsychDB](https://psychdb.com/ocd/z-other-oc-and-related-disorders); [Carlat Report on DSM-5 OCRD chapter](https://www.thecarlatreport.com/the-carlat-psychiatry-report/new-chapter-dsm-5-obsessive-compulsive-and-related-disorders)
- DSM-5-TR added olfactory reference disorder as a named example in this category. — [APA DSM-5-TR fact sheet](https://psychiatry.org/File%20Library/Psychiatrists/Practice/DSM/DSM-5-TR/APA-DSM5TR-OlfactoryReferenceDisorder.pdf)

**Trichoteiromania (rubbing-induced hair breakage)**
- Trichoteiromania is "a friction-induced hair shaft disorder caused by repetitive rubbing/scratching." It is often seen as the hair-shaft form of lichen simplex chronicus ("scratching pseudo-alopecia"). Itch or an urge to rub is common and helps tell it apart from trichotillomania, which is often painless and itch-free. — [Corona-Rodarte et al., Skin Appendage Disord 2026](https://doi.org/10.1159/000551364)
- Trüeb et al. say that trichotillomania is an OCD-spectrum disorder but **the underlying disorder in trichoteiromania varies between patients**. They add that "15 min of rubbing the hair is sufficient for hair breakage," and that trichotillomania, trichoteiromania, neurotic excoriations, factitious dermatitis, lichen simplex chronicus and prurigo nodularis of the scalp must be clearly separated. — [Trüeb et al., Int J Trichology 2025](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_7_25)
- **Three men with male-pattern AGA also had trichoscopic signs of trichoteiromania** ("patterned trichoteiromania"). The authors say self-induced hair loss should be considered at every follow-up of any alopecia, "remembering that hair loss itself is already a distressing condition." — [Sánchez-Dueñas et al., Skin Appendage Disord 2021](https://doi.org/10.1159/000519951)
- The self-inflicted hair disorders include trichotillomania (pulling), trichoteiromania (rubbing), trichotemnomania (cutting) and trichocryptomania/trichorrhexomania (breaking). — [Sánchez-Dueñas 2021](https://doi.org/10.1159/000519951)
- A case series of 5 calls trichoteiromania "a rare self-inflicting obsessive-compulsive disease." — [Bhat et al., Int J Trichology 2025](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_97_24)

**Lichen simplex chronicus / prurigo of the scalp**
- In a 40-case dermoscopy study of scalp prurigo nodularis/LSC, common findings were follicular plugs, broken hairs, purple structureless areas, broom-like hairs and erosions. — [Plozner et al., Dermatol Ther 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01672-7)

### Inferences
- A practical way to sort the differential:
  - **BDD** if the touching is driven by worry about how the hair looks ("checking whether it's thinner," feeling density, fixing it, comparing) and goes with mirror checking, photos, hats or reassurance seeking.
  - **OCD checking** if the driver is a non-appearance fear or a "just right" feeling.
  - **BFRB/trichoteiromania** if the behavior is automatic or soothing, triggered by boredom, stress or itch.
  - **LSC** if itch-scratch is the main driver.
- These can overlap. A BDD man may rub to check, and the rubbing then becomes a semi-automatic habit.
- Pulling the hair out points to trichotillomania. Rubbing or scratching without pulling points to trichoteiromania, or to excoriation disorder if he is picking scabs.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact DSM-5-TR wording of BDD Criterion B (which lists behaviors such as mirror checking, excessive grooming, skin picking, reassurance seeking and comparing). Whether "touching the area to check" is explicitly listed is unconfirmed here, though it is commonly described in the clinical literature.
- No sources were gathered on tic disorders or ADHD fidgeting as causes of hair touching. These remain theoretical differentials only.
- No sources found on the prevalence of trichoteiromania in men with AGA, beyond case reports.

## 2. How common is BDD in dermatology, hair-loss and hair-transplant patients? Hair-loss BDD in men; reassurance seeking

### Takeaway
BDD is several times more common in dermatology and cosmetic settings (about 12–15% in a meta-analysis) than in the general population (about 2%). No reliable pooled figure exists for hair-transplant clinics specifically. Thinning hair/balding is a characteristic male BDD concern, and reassurance seeking toward doctors is typical.

### Cited Findings
- In a meta-analysis of 33 studies, **12.65% of dermatology patients** (range 4.52–35.16%; mean age about 28) and **15.04% of plastic surgery patients** had BDD. — [Ribeiro, Aesthetic Plast Surg 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-017-0869-0)
- A 2025 meta-analysis found 24% BDD prevalence in plastic surgery patients and an "overall prevalence of BDD in the general population" of 17%. That figure is much higher than the roughly 2% from epidemiological studies, which likely reflects screening-tool definitions, so treat it with caution. — [Pérez-Buenfil & Morales-Sánchez, J Cosmet Dermatol 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70121); compare [Rück et al. 2024 (~2%)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41572-024-00577-z)
- In hair transplantation, screening tools such as the BDDQ and Beck Depression Inventory help flag high-risk people. Poor screening or patient selection "may result in dissatisfaction or worsening mental health." A multidisciplinary approach is recommended. — [Tan & Jafferany, J Cosmet Dermatol 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70475)
- A survey study of BDD in hair-transplant patients exists (south India), but only a letter with no abstract was retrievable. — [Hafi et al., Dermatol Ther 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/dth.13945)
- The BDDQ dermatology version showed 100% sensitivity and 93% specificity against a clinician interview (n=46). — [Dufresne et al., Dermatol Surg 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1524-4725.2001.00190.x)
- Dermatologists may be the practitioners most often consulted by people with BDD. — [Phillips & Dufresne 2000](https://doi.org/10.2165/00128071-200001040-00005)
- Lower self-esteem, teasing, abuse and stronger beliefs about the importance of attractiveness are associated with BDD, consistent with cognitive-behavioral models. Marriage was protective (RR 0.67). — [Toh et al., JAAD Int 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdin.2024.12.005)

### Inferences
- A 30-year-old man with hair loss who touches his hair compulsively belongs to a demographic (dermatology/hair clinic attendee, male, hair focus) where BDD should be actively screened, e.g., with the BDDQ-DV.

### Gaps
- No pooled prevalence of BDD specifically among hair-loss or hair-transplant patients was found. Individual studies exist but their figures were not retrieved.

## 3. How repetitive touching/rubbing/scratching damages the scalp and hair; trichoscopy

### Takeaway
Repeated friction breaks hair shafts mechanically. This shows up as broom/brush hairs, V-hairs, split ends, broken hairs of different lengths and trichorrhexis nodosa. Scratching adds excoriations, crusts, erythema, scale and lichenification. Trichoscopy can separate self-inflicted damage from alopecia areata and AGA, and both can coexist.

### Cited Findings
- **Trichoteiromania trichoscopy:** broom hairs, V-shaped hairs, short broken hairs of different lengths, perifollicular/interfollicular scale, and lichenified plaques with excoriations, erythema and haemorrhagic crusts. It was diagnosed without biopsy and treated with topical urea plus clobetasol and psychiatric referral. — [Corona-Rodarte et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1159/000551364)
- In 5 cases: broken hairs, longitudinal splits along the shaft (broom-like hairs; the most common finding), background erythema and white scales. — [Bhat et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_97_24)
- Friction can cause acquired **trichorrhexis nodosa** (nodes and transverse fractures, "two brooms stuck in opposite direction"). — [Jindal et al., Int J Trichology 2022](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_64_19)
- Brush-like ends/broom hairs alongside patterned miniaturization indicate trichoteiromania layered on AGA. — [Sánchez-Dueñas 2021](https://doi.org/10.1159/000519951)
- **Trichotillomania vs alopecia areata** (52 AA vs 23 TTM):
  - More common in TTM: broken hairs of different lengths (100% vs 3.8%), trichoptilosis (78.3% vs 5.8%), V-sign (43.5% vs 3.8%), flame hairs (43.5% vs 0%), hair powder.
  - More common in AA: exclamation-mark hairs (59.6% vs 26.1%), tapered hairs, yellow dots, angulated hairs.
  — [Khunkhet et al., Int J Dermatol 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijd.13453)
- Black dots, coiled/hook hairs, shafts of varying length with split ends, and the *absence* of exclamation-mark hairs and yellow dots suggest TTM. — [Martín et al., Pediatr Dermatol 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/pde.13756)
- Trüeb et al. caution that trichoscopy alone "can even be misleading" and must sit inside a full dermatologic and psychopathologic assessment. — [Trüeb et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_7_25)
- Scalp LSC/prurigo dermoscopy shows erosions, broken and broom-like hairs, and follicular plugs. — [Plozner et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01672-7)

### Inferences
- Self-inflicted breakage adds **visible** thinning on top of AGA. This can make the hair loss look worse than AGA alone, which feeds the worry.
- Breakage from friction is generally non-scarring and reversible once the behavior stops, unlike follicle loss from AGA. This is an inference consistent with these descriptions but not directly quantified.

### Gaps
- No sources gathered on secondary bacterial folliculitis, infection, or scarring from chronic scalp excoriation. These are plausible clinically, but I am not citing them.

## 4. Vicious cycle: hair-loss anxiety → checking → damage → more anxiety; self-esteem

### Takeaway
The literature supports each link: hair loss lowers self-esteem; BDD checking and reassurance-seeking behaviors keep preoccupation going; friction causes real breakage. No study was found that explicitly measures this loop in AGA.

### Cited Findings
- Hair loss causes "significant psychological distress and may exacerbate depression, anxiety, and social withdrawal." Hair is tied to self-esteem, identity and social functioning. — [Tan & Jafferany 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70475)
- Hair thinning and *perceived* hair loss bring self-consciousness, embarrassment, frustration and jealousy, and hair is a key part of body image. — [Dhami 2021](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0041-1741037)
- Lower self-esteem is associated with BDD, consistent with cognitive-behavioral models in which repetitive checking keeps the preoccupation going. — [Toh et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdin.2024.12.005)
- In BDD, people with high symptom levels had lower self-esteem and more psychopathology. — [Mulkens et al., JPRAS 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjps.2011.09.038)
- Hair loss itself is "already a distressing condition," and self-induced damage should be checked for in every alopecia. — [Sánchez-Dueñas 2021](https://doi.org/10.1159/000519951)
- Fifteen minutes of rubbing can break hair. — [Trüeb et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_7_25)

### Inferences
- The proposed loop: AGA → anxiety and self-esteem drop → frequent touching to "check" density (negative reinforcement: brief relief or certainty) → friction causes breakage, scaling and scabs → hair looks and feels worse → more anxiety and more checking.
- Reassurance (from the mirror, the hand, or other people) works like an OCD/BDD compulsion: short-term relief that keeps the cycle going. This is standard CBT theory, not sourced here specifically.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that quantifies this cycle in men with AGA.

## 5. Treatments

### Takeaway
- **BFRB/trichoteiromania-type behavior:** first-line is behavioral therapy (HRT, ComB, ACT- or DBT-enhanced). N-acetylcysteine, clomipramine and olanzapine each have RCT support, mostly in trichotillomania.
- **BDD:** CBT with ERP/ritual prevention, plus SSRIs, often at higher doses and for longer than in depression.
- Cosmetic procedures, including hair transplant, rarely improve BDD and should be postponed when BDD is suspected.
- Dermatologic care (topical steroid/urea for LSC-like lesions, AGA treatment) should run alongside psychiatric care: the psychodermatology approach.

### Cited Findings
**Behavioral therapy for BFRBs**
- A meta-analysis of 24 RCTs (857 participants) in trichotillomania found **behavioral therapy with HRT components had the largest effect (SMD −1.22)**. Clomipramine (SMD −0.71), **N-acetylcysteine (SMD −0.75)** and olanzapine (SMD −0.94) beat placebo, but each rests on single trials that need replication. No FDA-approved first-line drug exists. — [Farhat et al., Depress Anxiety 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.23028)
- HRT (Azrin & Nunn, 1970s) has three critical components:
  - awareness training;
  - competing response, e.g., clenching fists or tensing the arms when the urge comes;
  - social support.
  HRT is supported for short-term gains, but long-term gains are harder to keep with HRT alone. CBT is described as superior to medication. — [TLC Foundation for BFRBs](https://www.bfrb.org/post/evidence-based-therapeutic-treatment-for-bfrbs)
- **ComB** (Mansueto and colleagues) assumes the behavior meets a need. It assesses five domains (Sensory, Cognitive, Affective, Motor, Place) and tailors strategies to them. TLC favors ComB, though empirical evaluation so far is limited. ACT-enhanced behavior therapy (Woods) and DBT-enhanced behavior therapy (Keuthen) outperformed controls, with benefits lasting months. — [TLC Foundation](https://www.bfrb.org/post/evidence-based-therapeutic-treatment-for-bfrbs); [IOCDF blog](https://iocdf.org/blog/2024/03/07/evidence-based-therapeutic-treatment-for-bfrbs/); [ADAA on ComB](https://adaa.org/learn-from-us/from-the-experts/blog-posts/professional/tackling-picking-and-pulling-all-angles-comb)

**Self-help and digital tools**
- In RCTs of self-help, self-guided HRT, **decoupling** and decoupling in sensu consistently reduced BFRB symptoms and improved quality of life. The "Free from BFRB" website was the most studied resource. — [Murase et al., Dermatol Ther 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-025-01380-8)
- In an RCT of self-help (n=141), HRT did not cause more negative effects, such as "rebound," than not using it. 70% of participants reported a positive effect. — [Baumeister et al., Behav Ther 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beth.2023.05.013)

**N-acetylcysteine**
- **Grant, Odlaug & Kim 2009** ran a 12-week double-blind RCT in 50 adults with trichotillomania (45 women, 5 men; mean age 34) using NAC 1200–2400 mg/day. **56% were "much or very much improved" on NAC vs 16% on placebo (p=.003).** Improvement first appeared at week 9, and there were no adverse events. NAC is thought to act by restoring glutamate in the nucleus accumbens. — [Grant et al., Arch Gen Psychiatry 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2009.60)
- A systematic review of lifestyle interventions found that 4 of 6 NAC studies (in TTM, skin picking and OCD) showed promising results. — [Brierley et al., Psychosom Med 2021](https://doi.org/10.1097/PSY.0000000000000988)

**Medication evidence across psychodermatologic disorders**
- Across 21 RCTs, the evidence supports:
  - sertraline and clomipramine for trichotillomania;
  - fluoxetine for skin picking;
  - olanzapine for trichotillomania;
  - NAC for trichotillomania and skin picking.
  Lamotrigine, inositol and milk thistle were also tested. — [Turk et al., J Cutan Med Surg 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/12034754231155888)

**BDD treatment**
- First-line BDD treatment is CBT plus serotonin reuptake inhibitors, but many patients do not reach full remission. — [Geho et al., JAAPA 2026](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.JAA.0000000000000350); [Rück et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41572-024-00577-z)
- A meta-analysis of 46 studies (N=2,227) on depressive symptoms in BDD found SSRIs (SMD −1.16) and CBT (SMD −1.12; −0.65 after bias correction) both had large effects. Combined treatment may be best. — [Loewen et al., Curr Psychiatry Rep 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11920-026-01688-y)
- **BDD often needs higher SRI doses and longer trials than other disorders**:
  - A 2021 international consensus: "high doses of SSRI and protracted periods."
  - One guidance: an adequate trial is 12–16 weeks, with at least 4 weeks at the maximum dose. Some patients respond only above the maximum labeled dose (e.g., citalopram or paroxetine 80–100 mg/day, specialist use).
  - Next steps if an SSRI fails: augmentation (buspirone, atypical antipsychotic), switching SSRI, or clomipramine. No RCTs define the next best step.
  — secondary summaries via [PMC9063569 (international consensus)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9063569/); [MDedge](https://www.mdedge.com/content/im-ugly-elephant-man-how-recognize-and-treat-body-dysmorphic-disorder/page/0/2). These were not opened directly.
- For pickers, simply telling them to "stop" doesn't work. Psychoeducation that the behavior is a treatable symptom helps. Patients should be referred to psychiatry for CBT or SRIs rather than to another dermatologist or surgeon. — [Phillips & Dufresne 2000](https://doi.org/10.2165/00128071-200001040-00005)

**Cosmetic treatment in BDD**
- Of 200 people with BDD, 71% sought and 64% received nonpsychiatric treatment, mostly dermatologic, then surgery. **Such treatment "rarely improved" BDD.** — [Crerand et al., Psychosomatics 2005](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.psy.46.6.549)
- Among cosmetic surgery patients, those with high BDD symptoms were more dissatisfied with results, had more psychopathology and had lower self-esteem. — [Mulkens et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjps.2011.09.038)
- Cosmetic and surgical procedures "rarely improve core BDD symptoms." They are linked to persistent dissatisfaction, symptom worsening and legal risk, and should be avoided when BDD is suspected, with referral to mental health prioritized. Regulators increasingly require psychological screening before cosmetic procedures. — [Gumma & Gumma, Aesthetic Plast Surg 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-026-05736-z)
- Hair transplant can improve self-esteem *when expectations are managed and psychological risk is screened*. Poor selection can worsen mental health. — [Tan & Jafferany 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70475)

**Combined dermatologic and psychiatric care**
- A trichoteiromania case was handled with reassurance, topical urea plus clobetasol, and psychiatric follow-up for the rubbing. — [Corona-Rodarte et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1159/000551364)
- Combined pharmacotherapy and psychotherapy gave a "very good clinical response" in a trichoteiromania case series. — [Bhat et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijt.ijt_97_24)

### Inferences
- When to see whom:
  - **Dermatologist/trichologist:** diagnose AGA, look for trichoteiromania/LSC/folliculitis with trichoscopy, treat the scalp lesions and the AGA.
  - **Psychiatrist/psychologist:** BDD screening is positive; daily life is impaired (e.g., touching every minute, time lost, avoidance); there is depression or suicidal thinking (BDD carries elevated suicide risk).
  - **Before any hair transplant:** screen for BDD (e.g., BDDQ-DV).
- HRT/ComB fits automatic or soothing touching. ERP (resisting the check, dropping reassurance seeking, cutting mirror checking) fits touching that is a BDD/OCD-style check. Many patients need both.

### Gaps
- No RCT evidence was retrieved for memantine in BFRBs, or for specific BFRB tracking apps (e.g., HabitAware). I did not verify these.
- No Cochrane review was found or opened in this round. (A Cochrane review of BDD treatment, Ipser et al. 2009, exists to my knowledge but was not verified here.)
- The source evidence for NAC and the HRT meta-analysis is in trichotillomania, mostly in women. Extrapolating it to male hair rubbing/checking is an inference.
- No trials were found specifically in trichoteiromania; the evidence is case reports and series only.
